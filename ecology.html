An Arctic Oasis

Birding Potential

First recognized by Europeans for it's importance to migratory birds, (Parks Canada Agency, 2025) Sirmilik National Park has more than 70 different species of birds that migrate or live on Bylot Island. The lush arctic wetland is an important stopping point for Greater Snow Geese, with over 70,000 birds spending their summer on the island. (Cadieux et.al.,2006) The Greater Snow Geese population drastically rose With the increase supply of food on their migratory route between Sirmilik National Park, the St. Lawrence river valley and the mid-atlantic United States thanks to large-scale agriculture. This created a need to manage the Snow Geese population to prevent them from disrupting the otherwise fragile artic ecosystem. Efforts are taken by the Bylot Island Monitoring Project and the Canadian Wildlife Agency to manage the area outside of the breeding season to limit the Goose population. (Cadieux et.al.,2006)
