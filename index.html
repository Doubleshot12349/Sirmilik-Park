Overview

An arctic wilderness, featuring glaciers, valleys, and cliffs. Located near Baffin Island's on the northeastern coast of Canada's arctic circle, Sirmilik National Park has a stark dazzling landscape, along with wildlife, seabirds, polar bears, seals, and whales which thrive in the area. These have allowed the local Inuit people to thrive here for thousands of years. (Parks Canada Agency, 2025)

History

Beginning in 1700 B.C.E until about 1000 C.E the Dorset and Thule people, ancestors of modern Inuits arrived in modern-day Alaska across the Bering Strait before gradually migrating across Canada. (Parks Canada Agency, 2025) They adapted to be semi-nomadic amphibious hunters, using dog sleds and boats to hunt caribou, seals, fish, and water fowl. Species still present in what would become Sirmilik National Park.

In modern times the European explorer, J.E. Bernier, took possession of Bylot Island for Canada, marvelling over the diverse migratory bird population. Although the area wouldn't begin to be managed by Canada's Government and the bird population studied officially until 1950, when Bylot Island Migratory Bird Sanctuary was founded. (Parks Canada Agency, 2025)

Nearby, Pond Inlet saw explorer trading posts following an unsuccessful gold rush in 1912. Here the Inuit continued their way of life until 1960, when their children were forced to attend schools. (Parks Canada Agency, 2025) Inuit people would continue to live in and assist with management of the park as it is expanded to it's present boundaries in 2001, along with Parks Canada and the Canadian Wildlife Agency. (National Geographic, 2011)
