Practices

In recognition of Inuit knowledge about the land and wildlife, Parks Canada has committed to incorporating Traditional Ecological Knowledge (TEK) into the management of Sirmilik and many other national parks. This involves collaborating with and collecting indigenous knowledge, especially about greater snow geese and red/arctic Foxes. (Cadieux et.al.,2006) Inuit are also expected to get economic benefit from the park, including having priority to be hired and contracted by Parks Canada to work in the park and run tours. The park is co-managed by a committee including Inuit members, as well as government employees, carrying out a survey about the success of the parks management and Inuit use of the park every 7 years. Finally Inuit people do not pay an entry fee to visit the park, , and may continue to exercise their hunting rights within the park up to a set Total Allowable Harvest, managed by an Inuit Hunting Organization. (Nunavut Tunngavik Incorporated,1993)

Funding

Sirmilik National Park is funded with tax dollars from the Government of Canada provided to Parks Canada for the management of the park. (Nunavut Tunngavik Incorporated,1993) Additional funding is collected from visitors as they pay an entry fee, and acquire permits to establish base camps, conduct research, and commercial photography, among other items. (Parks Canada Agency, 2025) The Inuit primarily profit off of visitors to the park booking expedition guides and selling wildlife merchandise. (Nunavut Tunngavik Incorporated,1993)

Monitoring

Wildlife populations are monitored by scientists, Inuit, and Parks Canada to ensure the ecosystem's stability. This is done through trapping, as well as searching for activity within dens and nests. (Cadieux et.al.,2006) Additionally visitors to the park are encouraged to take pictures and report sightings of wildlife to assist in monitoring their populations. (Parks Canada Agency, 2025) Primarily the greater snow geese population must be monitored to ensure their high population doesn't destabilize the delicate arctic ecosystem, while foxes, narwhals, bowhead whales, among others are monitored to ensure their populations remain healty. (Cadieux et.al.,2006)
